01 / DISCOVER KB-09

A personal synth, ready to explore

KB-09 recreates the character of Kevin’s Roland SH-09 in a hands-on software instrument. A main oscillator, sub oscillator, resonant low-pass filter and envelope shape everything from deep bass to singing leads and noise percussion. Its signal path follows the SH-09 circuit design; the digital model reproduces how those stages shape and interact with the sound.

Start with one of 24 factory sounds, then make it your own. AGE changes the instrument’s appearance and calibration, while External Input and its envelope follower let incoming guitar, drums or another synth control the filter. Play in your DAW or use the standalone with a MIDI keyboard. Explore the filter model.

02 / INSIDE THE SOUND

The hardest sound to get right

The resonant filter was KB-09's biggest sound-design challenge. At high RESONANCE, the SH-09's filter becomes an oscillator in its own right. Add a steady VCO and sweep the filter slowly, and two sources of energy interact inside the circuit. Recreating that balance meant capturing a singing, stable resonance while retaining the movement and character of the hardware.

How we matched the SH-09 — recordings and waveform comparisons

The crucial test put a steady VCO into a self-oscillating filter while its pitch swept slowly past the VCO’s harmonics. The two signals interacted inside the filter, revealing whether its tone stayed steady, changed level or was pulled toward the incoming oscillator. We compared a collection of SH-09 and KB-09 recordings at different VCO mixer levels, alongside filter-only and oscillator-only recordings, captured at 96 kHz in 32-bit floating point. Listening, waveforms and frequency analysis guided the model toward the hardware’s balance of stable resonance and musical interaction. Matching pitch and relative signal levels made those comparisons more useful than copying slider positions alone.

The model gives each filter stage an appropriate amount of internal signal headroom and controls the resonance with a separate nonlinear feedback limiter. The stages and limiter are solved together at each internal processing step. This helps the filter sustain its own tone as the incoming VCO changes, while preserving their interaction. Self-oscillation comes from the filter's feedback loop, with no separate sine generator or automatic volume correction.

Listening checks were paired with tests of tuning, ordinary patch levels, stability and processing cost across sample rates. The aim was a faithful, playable instrument: steady where the hardware is steady, expressive where its signals interact, and responsive under the fingers.

These comparisons show one resonant setting, not every possible patch. Individual SH-09s vary with calibration and condition, and Kevin's instrument has decades of wear. His recordings provide a real musical reference; they are not assumed to define factory specifications. Natural beating and harmonic interaction remain part of the sound.

03 / SETUP

Install & open

Requirements, installation and first-play instructions for Windows.

No DAW is required. Each download includes a standalone app. The synth and manual work offline; internet access is only needed for downloads, update checks, bug reports and external links.

Requirements

64-bit Windows 10 or 11 on an Intel/AMD x64 processor; tested on Windows 11. The download includes a standalone EXE and VST3 for compatible 64-bit DAWs.

A comfortable starting point

Windows 11; a four-core Intel Core i5-8250U or AMD Ryzen 5 2500U class processor or better; 8 GB RAM for standalone use, or 16 GB or more for DAW projects. Strong single-core performance matters more than a large core count for one instrument. In a DAW, use your audio interface manufacturer’s ASIO driver when available.

Allow at least 1 GB free for downloading, extracting and installing. An SSD and a 1920 × 1080 or larger display are recommended; smaller displays can use S. These are practical recommendations, not certified minimums. Your DAW’s requirements also apply.

Audio setup: begin at 44.1 or 48 kHz with a 256-sample buffer. Try 512 if audio crackles, or 128 if playing feels delayed and your computer has room to spare. Higher sample rates and multiple instances increase CPU load. KB-09 reports 128 samples of processing latency, which compatible DAWs compensate for during playback. Wired headphones or an audio interface are preferable to Bluetooth for responsive playing.

Keep the plug-in sharp: in Ableton Live, right-click KB-09 in the Browser and turn off Auto-Scale Plug-in Window if offered, then reopen its editor. Use KB-09’s S/M/L buttons for size. Other hosts may have a similar bitmap-scaling option; see your DAW’s instructions. Ableton’s display guidance.

  4. Reopen your DAW and find Toyland KB-09 under instruments. Rescan VST3 plug-ins if needed. The installer uses the standard VST3 location.

For a portable desktop shortcut, right-click the EXE and choose Show more options > Send to > Desktop (create shortcut). Keep the extracted folder in a permanent location.

Standalone: play immediately

  1. Connect your MIDI controller by USB, or connect its MIDI OUT to your audio interface’s MIDI IN.
  2. Click AUDIO / MIDI. Select an audio output and enable your controller under Active MIDI inputs; scroll inside the settings if necessary. Close the settings and load 01 / Calibrated Init.

If it will not enable, close other apps using that MIDI port. Choose a shared audio device if another app occupies an exclusive driver.

04 / FIRST SOUND

Start playing in a minute

Play your first sound
  1. Open the standalone app, or install the version for your computer and load Toyland KB-09 on a DAW instrument/MIDI track.
  2. Choose 01 / Calibrated Init in the preset field along the top.
  3. Turn down your listening level, play a MIDI keyboard (enable MIDI monitoring if using a DAW), or enable KEYBOARD in the standalone app. You can also click KB-09's keys.
  4. Move CUTOFF FREQ to hear the sound darken or brighten. Add a little RESONANCE for a stronger filter peak.
  5. Try 02 / Fundamental Bass or 04 / Singing Lead, then save a sound of your own.

KB-09 is monophonic: it plays one note at a time, with the lowest held note taking priority. Overlapping notes and the envelope trigger switch shape its legato behavior. Click and hold an onscreen key, or hold a MIDI note, to see the key depress and darken. A small amber bar along the key's lower edge identifies the active note; other held keys remain shaded without a colored marker. The marks disappear when the keys are released.

No DAW? Use the standalone app. Toyland KB-09 Standalone is a complete playable virtual synth. Choose your audio output and MIDI input in its audio/MIDI settings, then select a preset and play. It does not record a song or replace a DAW.

05 / CHARACTER

Meet AGE

Choose the condition of your instrument.

CONDITION / AGE lives below the instrument. At 1 / Minty it selects the healthy calibration profile. Turn it up to explore a more played-in instrument, then turn it back whenever you like. AGE is saved with your sound and works in both Authentic and Modern modes.

Click a marked AGE position or drag its cap to select one of five conditions. Each appearance has one name:

The five AGE conditions
  1. Minty — fresh and calibrated.
  2. Seen Some Shit
  3. Vintage, Baby!
  4. Sold As-Is
  5. Fire Hazard — the most worn condition.

The five marked settings run from 1 to 5, with one appearance and name per setting. AGE is saved with your patch.

Inspired by Kevin's own aged SH-09, AGE changes both the synth's appearance and its circuit behavior.

What changes?

Appearance

Keys and printed legends yellow, keys become scuffed and uneven, and knobs lose their shine. Slider caps twist, lean and go missing. Dust, tarnish and patches of rust build up around the panel, while the side cheeks collect scratches and gouges. At Fire Hazard, the bender pointer breaks off. Silver trim dulls and pits without rusting.

The controls continue to work through every condition. Lower AGE whenever you want to bring back the caps, intact bender and fresher panel.

Sound

AGE gradually shifts pitch calibration, filter tuning, resonance and envelope timing. The same setting gives the same condition each time, so it can be saved with a patch.

AGE is a creative condition control rather than a clock counting elapsed years. It does not add random crackles or make a worn key stop playing.

Try this

Save a favorite patch. Hold one note and move AGE slowly from fresh to worn. Compare the subtle sound changes, then explore the filter and envelope with AGE at a setting you like. Inspect the panel at L size, and load your saved patch whenever you want to return to the starting point.

Play the filter itself

Lower VCO, SUB OSC and EXT to zero, set VCA to HOLD, lower VCF MOD and ENV, then raise RESONANCE until the filter sings. CUTOFF FREQ sets its pitch; the keyboard and VCF bender depth also move it. Start with a low listening level.

About the SH-09-inspired filter model

The original Roland service schematic guides the four-stage filter and its resonance routing. KB-09 uses four nonlinear low-pass stages with instantaneous feedback and a separate nonlinear resonance limiter. Self-oscillation grows in that feedback loop, rather than a separate sine generator. The sound engine processes at four times the host sample rate, with band-limited oscillator edges and filtered downsampling.

This is a circuit-informed approximation, not a component-calibrated recreation of the BA662 stages or their limiting diodes. AGE is a creative condition model, not measured decades of wear. See the original Roland service notes.

Authentic & Modern

Authentic keeps the original-style routing and ignores the optional velocity, aftertouch, LFO tempo-sync and input-trim settings. Those controls dim. Modern enables them: velocity controls level, channel aftertouch opens the filter (up to three octaves at full pressure and AFTERTOUCH amount), FREE extends RATE down to one cycle every 20 seconds; SYNC locks RATE to musical grid divisions, and INPUT TRIM adjusts external input by ±24 dB. Return to Modern to use their retained settings.

Modern provides deeper maximum LFO modulation: ±36 semitones for VCO pitch and ±8 octaves for the VCF, compared with Authentic's ±12 semitones and ±5 octaves. These are software design ranges. The bender sensitivities are separate.

The header's ENV and EXT indicators show the synth envelope and external envelope-follower activity, respectively. EXT AUDIO opens a routing reminder in the same dialog style as the other KB-09 windows; choose OK to close.

07 / YOUR SOUNDS

Save your sounds

  1. Adjust the panel, including AGE and the Authentic/Modern choice.
  2. Click SAVE, choose a location you can find again and give the sound a name. KB-09 writes a .kb09 file.
  3. To recall it, click LOAD and select that .kb09 file.

SAVE starts in Documents / Toyland KB-09 Presets, with writable copies of all twenty-four factory patches. Choose another folder whenever you like. LOAD starts in the folder where you most recently saved a patch, even after reopening KB-09. Loading a patch from elsewhere does not change that default.

Closing the standalone: choose Save, Don't Save, or Cancel. Use the arrow keys to switch the highlighted Save / Don't Save / Cancel choice, Enter to choose it, or Esc to cancel. Save lets you name a .kb09 file and opens that saved patch next launch. Don't Save starts the next launch with Calibrated Init. Cancel leaves the app open; canceling the save dialog or a failed save also leaves it open. If the saved startup file is moved or deleted, KB-09 falls back to Calibrated Init. DAW projects continue to restore their own plug-in state.

The loaded filename appears in the preset field. The twenty-four factory presets remain available; selecting one recalls its original settings. Save your edits before choosing another sound. Canceling a file dialog or rejecting an invalid preset leaves the current sound unchanged.

A preset saves the sound parameters, including AGE. It does not save held notes, the current envelope position, power or window placement. Your DAW also saves the patch with its project; external track routing belongs to that DAW project.

10 / BEYOND THE OSCILLATOR

External audio: an envelope filter

Envelope filter is the precise term. ENV FOL'R follows the loudness of incoming audio and uses it to move the low-pass cutoff: harder playing opens the filter further. This level-responsive effect is often called auto-wah. It differs from an LFO-driven wah, whose movement follows the modulator rather than your playing.

KB-09's VST3 instrument exposes an External Input auxiliary audio bus. A DAW may call this a sidechain. Here the incoming audio can become part of the sound, passing through the mixer, filter and amplifier.

  1. Route an audio source to the instrument's External Input.
  2. Choose 12 / External Filter. It lowers the oscillators, raises EXT and uses VCA Hold, so no MIDI note is needed.
  3. Adjust CUTOFF FREQ and RESONANCE.
  4. Try 13 / External Follower: louder input opens the filter for an envelope-filter effect that responds to the input’s loudness. Adjust the VCF ENV amount and the source level.

For envelope filtering, the follower listens before the EXT mixer slider. Lowering EXT can make the source quieter while its filter modulation remains active. The follower does not trigger ADSR or open the VCA; use Hold for continuous processing. Stereo input is averaged to mono, so opposite-polarity channels can cancel. Output is mono duplicated to stereo.

The same envelope-follower idea appears in the original SH-09 owner's manual (external-instrument examples). KB-09 brings that expressive idea into your software setup. Try short guitar chords or a drum loop, lower CUTOFF, add RESONANCE and raise ENV until each attack opens the filter.

In Ableton Live

  1. Place KB-09 on a MIDI track; put the source audio on another track.
  2. Expand the plug-in device's Sidechain panel. Enable Sidechain, choose the source under Audio From and select the intended tap, such as Post FX.
  3. Set the sidechain mix to 100% for a known starting point, then choose External Filter in KB-09.
  4. Monitor the processed path clearly. Prevent the source's direct output from masking the filtered result.
  5. Save the Live Set to retain the track routing, then reopen it and verify the source and patch.

See the Live 11 sidechain routing guide. Menu names may vary in later versions. In another DAW, use its auxiliary-input or sidechain routing to send audio into KB-09's External Input.

Sounds, settings and ideas to return to:

Modern TUNE range

In MODERN mode, TUNE spans one octave down to one octave up (−12 to +12 semitones). Double-click for concert pitch at center. AUTHENTIC keeps the original ±65-cent fine-tuning range. Your DAW may display the stored tuning value rather than semitones; the knob’s value bubble shows the effective pitch change.

Play VCO MOD from your mod wheel

Your controller’s modulation wheel (MIDI CC1) controls the VCO MOD fader in both AUTHENTIC and MODERN modes. The fader follows the wheel, changing the amount of LFO pitch modulation; RATE and the selected LFO waveform determine the movement. Each mode keeps its existing depth range.

In a VST3 host, the wheel controls VCO MOD on its next movement, including after loading a sound or changing the fader. Mouse edits and host automation remain available; the next wheel message takes control again. The standalone uses pickup to prevent jumps: move the wheel to or through the fader’s position after a manual edit or preset change. The resulting fader setting is saved with the patch. Record wheel gestures as MIDI CC1, or use the VCO Modulation parameter for DAW automation. In a DAW, other MIDI knobs and faders use the host’s mapping system. The standalone offers MIDI MAP for custom assignments; assigning CC1 elsewhere replaces its normal VCO MOD function until that assignment is cleared.

Map your controller in standalone

Click MIDI MAP, select a highlighted KB-09 control, tap a physical button or move a knob/fader, then click DONE. For a momentary button, release it before DONE; a toggle button needs only one press. When assigning a knob/fader to a switch, move through its middle so KB-09 recognizes direct positioning.

Buttons assigned to controls with three or more positions cycle one position per press. Knobs and faders select positions directly. Continuous controls use pickup: cross the onscreen position to take control without a jump. OPTIONS provides a correction for unusual button behavior.

CLEAR SELECTED removes one assignment; CLEAR ALL asks for confirmation before resetting them all. Mappings remember the MIDI channel and are saved between launches, separately from sound presets. They support absolute CC 0–119 on channels 1–16; set relative encoders to absolute mode. Pitch bend, notes and channel aftertouch keep their normal functions.

Enable DESCRIPTION for hover help. In a DAW, use the host’s mapping system; MIDI MAP is available only in standalone.

Pitch-bender readout

Hover over or drag the spring-return bender to see its signed position, from -100% to +100%; center is 0%. The VCO and VCF depth faders determine the resulting pitch and filter movement, so the position is not a fixed semitone amount. Release the onscreen bender for a short, 50 ms return in AUTHENTIC mode at constant speed, stopping at center without easing; sound and animation return together. MODERN returns immediately, retaining click-free audio smoothing. The spring duration is a playing approximation, not a measured SH-09 calibration. An external MIDI wheel moves the GUI bender and follows the controller’s own return messages. In a DAW, record or edit MIDI Pitch Bend envelopes; the bender position is a MIDI performance control, while its VCO and VCF depth faders are automatable plug-in parameters.

A little more control, a lot more playing

Hold SHIFT while dragging any knob, fader or the bender for ten-times finer adjustment. Release SHIFT to return to normal speed, even during a drag. AGE selects five marked conditions while dragging. Five cached appearances keep movement responsive; full visual detail returns on release. During automation, control positions and key feedback may stay at the last complete preview while you drag AGE; they return live on release. The four utility sliders have larger handles and grab areas. Faders move relative to where you grab them, so a fine adjustment will not jump to the pointer. The DESCRIPTION button in the bottom strip enables hover descriptions; it starts off and lights amber with DESCRIPTION ON when enabled. Hover over DESCRIPTION itself for help even when it is off. Value readouts work with DESCRIPTION off or on. Switches show their selected mode in a readable value box when hovered or adjusted; the box disappears after you leave or finish the adjustment.

VOLUME has an audio taper: quiet levels occupy useful knob travel, 0 mutes, and 10 is full output.

In Modern, choose FREE for the continuous 0.05–25 Hz LFO, or SYNC to follow the host tempo. RATE then selects 21 divisions, from four bars to 1/64-note triplets. Its value box displays the division. D means dotted (one-and-a-half times the straight duration); T means triplet (two-thirds). Bar labels assume 4/4; divisions are quarter-note beat durations and do not change with the host time signature. Without host tempo information the clock uses 120 BPM. Authentic always uses the original free rate, even with a saved SYNC setting.

The RATE lamp follows the LFO clock with clear half-cycle flashes below 8 Hz. At 8 Hz and above it becomes a steady glow that brightens with rate: this avoids misleading flicker when a screen cannot show each cycle. Modulation and LFO envelope triggering continue at the actual selected rate. SYNC follows tempo, not the host transport's bar position; stopping playback does not reset its phase.

The header's IN meter shows audio reaching the plug-in before the AUDIO MIXER's EXT fader and before stereo is folded to mono. ENV shows the synth envelope; EXT shows the external envelope follower. MIDI flashes when notes, pitch bend or other MIDI channel messages arrive. Enable DESCRIPTION and hover over an indicator for an explanation. IN activity with silence at the output means you should check EXT level, VCA mode and filter cutoff. Opposite-polarity stereo channels can light IN yet cancel in the mono signal.

When an update is available, choose Show update instructions or Not now. Updates are optional. Installation is manual and usually takes a few minutes, depending on download speed. The instructions offer Open download folder and Copy instructions; both leave the instructions open. Copy them before closing KB-09. No update downloads or installs itself.

Your saved presets and audio/MIDI preferences are kept. Save your current patch before closing KB-09. The normal preset folder is Documents/Toyland KB-09 Presets. Keep personal presets stored inside an old extracted download folder before replacing it.

  1. Download the Windows ZIP and extract it completely. Keep personal presets stored inside the old folder before replacing it.
  2. Save your patch and close KB-09. Close your DAW too if using plug-ins.
  3. For a portable copy, replace the extracted folder and open the new standalone. For an installed copy, rerun INSTALL KB-09.cmd; it also offers the standalone desktop shortcut.
  4. Confirm the new version under TOYLAND / KB-09. Reopen your DAW and rescan plug-ins if necessary.

Update-check preferences belong to your operating-system user account, apply across projects, and are not stored in presets. Failed automatic checks stay quiet. A failed manual check shows a retry message.

12 / QUICK FIXES

Solve a problem

In the standalone app, Ctrl+W closes the window through the same Save / Don’t Save / Cancel prompt as the close button.

I cannot find KB-09 in my DAW

Extract the download, run INSTALL KB-09.cmd, and rescan VST3 plug-ins in your music software. Look under instruments. Use a 64-bit VST3 host.

I hear nothing

Load Calibrated Init, switch POWER on and play a fresh note. Check track monitoring, MIDI input, VOLUME and the DAW output. Choose OK to close the EXT AUDIO dialog before playing. External presets need an audio source. In standalone, check audio/MIDI settings.

A sound keeps playing

VCA HOLD deliberately passes audio without a key. Try ENV or GATE, or load Calibrated Init. POWER mutes and clears note history; play a new note after turning it back on.

The display is blurry or low resolution

Use the DAW-specific scaling setting. In Live, disabling Auto-Scale Plug-in Window fixed this for the owner. Then choose KB-09's own size. Do not lower the screen resolution to make the plug-in bigger.

The footer or a control is outside the window

Choose S or M, reopen the editor and check the host's scaling setting. Try a fresh instance after saving the patch. If it persists, note the DAW version, Windows display scale, monitor resolution and selected size when reporting the problem; a host can constrain a plug-in's requested dimensions.

Velocity, aftertouch, sync or input trim does nothing

Select Modern. Authentic deliberately ignores these optional controls. Aftertouch opens the filter; its slider sets the amount. Try a moderately low CUTOFF and preset 24 / Aftertouch Brass. It needs a controller that sends channel pressure, rather than polyphonic aftertouch. Onscreen mouse presses do not provide expressive aftertouch.

External audio is quiet or disappears

Confirm the host enabled the auxiliary input, raise EXT and use VCA Hold. Stereo sources with opposite-polarity channels cancel when folded to mono. In Modern, check INPUT TRIM. IN shows the incoming audio peak before mono folding and mixer level; EXT shows the external envelope follower. These meters do not promise that your output path is audible.

My MIDI fader jumps or controls more than one thing in Live

A controller's Live preset or control-surface script can already assign a fader to track gain or another function. Avoid assigning that same MIDI channel/CC to two destinations: remove conflicting manual mappings, and use a separate controller user preset/channel for KB-09 controls if needed. For absolute faders, Live's Pickup or Value Scaling takeover mode can reduce jumps. KB-09 automatically uses CC1 for VCO MOD in a DAW; other controller faders are mapped by the host. In standalone, use MIDI MAP for custom assignments. Avoid simultaneously recording CC1 and automating VCO Modulation unless that is intentional. See Ableton's MIDI mapping guide.

Windows reports a missing VCRUNTIME or MSVCP file

Install Microsoft's current x64 Visual C++ Redistributable from Microsoft's official download page, then reopen the synth. Do not download individual DLLs from unrelated sites.
